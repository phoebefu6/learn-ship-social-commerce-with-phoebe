What the model is worth

Somebody will show you a model with a big number on the slide. Tonight you learn to ask for three smaller ones. Serai Commerce, the constructed enabler from session 1, built one repeat-purchase model on its real Olist orders: who is likely to order again within 90 days. In the month it never saw, 24 of 574 orders were followed by a repeat. The model's top tenth held 9 of them. That is the whole story of what the model is worth, and also why you should hold it loosely: one hit more or less moves the headline by 0.41. You leave with a one-page "what is this model worth" sheet and the questions that catch a model that looks too good.

Part 1 · covers base rate, AUC and lift at the top decile

Base rate first

6 min live

A model that "finds repeat buyers" is only worth something against what you would find without it. So the first number to ask for is not the model's: it is how many repeat buyers there are anyway. Everything else is a multiple of that.

LiveThree numbers, in business words3 min▶
NumberSerai, month T+1The question it answersWhat it does not tell you
Base rate4.18% (24 of 574)How many repeat buyers would a random contact list find?Anything about the model
AUC0.7894If you pick one repeat buyer and one non-repeat at random, how often does the model rank the repeat buyer higher?How many repeats you find in the slice you can afford to contact
Lift at the top decile3.71x (9 of the top 58)If you contact only the top tenth, how many times more repeats do you find than at random?Whether the contact caused the repeat

scikit-learn describes its AUC function plainly: "Compute Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (ROC AUC) from prediction scores." It is a ranking score across the whole list. A CRM team never contacts the whole list; it contacts the top of it. That is why the number on Serai's scorecard is lift at the top decile, and AUC sits beside it as a sanity check.

LiveThe decision the model changes2 min▶

A model is worth exactly the decision it changes. For Serai the decision is narrow: which customers a brand's CRM team contacts this month. Without the model the team contacts at random, or contacts everyone, and about 4 in 100 contacts land on someone who was going to order again. With the model the team contacts the top tenth and about 15 or 16 in 100 do. The top 58 orders hold 9 of the month's 24 repeats, 37.5% of them, from a tenth of the list.

Serai's constructed gate for the model row is a month T+1 lift of at least 2.0x. In this month that means the top 58 must hold at least 4.85 repeats, so in practice 5 hits passes and 4 does not (arithmetic on the bench's 4.18% base rate). If no decision changes when the model goes live, the model is a report, and reports do not carry 35% of a product's price.

Self-studySmall numbers: one hit is 0.413 min▶

Month T+1 has 574 orders and 24 real repeats. The top decile is 58 orders. Each repeat in those 58 is worth 1 / 58 of hit rate, which divided by the 4.18% base rate is 0.41 of lift. Nine hits read 3.71x; eight would read 3.30x; ten would read about 4.12x. So the honest way to say the result is "roughly three and a half to four times the base rate, on 24 positives", not "3.71x".

This is not a flaw of Serai's model; it is what a single month of a mid-sized enabler looks like. Two habits follow. Never compare two models on the third decimal of lift when the month has two dozen positives. And before you sign, ask for the same lift on a second unseen month: if it holds on fresh data, you believe it more; if it swings by a full point, you have learned the size of the noise.

Real world

Not every "repeat" is a second visit. In Serai's real Olist rows, 197 orders were followed by another order from the same customer within 90 days, and 83 of those repeats were placed on the same day. Some are surely split baskets rather than a customer coming back. The label counts them all as repeats because the data cannot tell them apart. That ambiguity sits inside every lift number on this page, and it is one more reason to read lift as a range rather than a point.

Part 2 · covers the time split and why only the next month counts

Only the next month counts

6 min live

A model is used on next month's customers, so it has to be judged on next month's customers. Anything measured on the months it trained on describes how well it memorised, not how well it will work when the CRM team presses send.

LiveThe split, in dates3 min▶

The label is "did this customer order again within 90 days". A 90-day label needs 90 days to become true or false. So the last training purchase is 2017-11-02, which lets its window close by 2018-01-31, the end of month T. The model is then scored on February 2018, month T+1, with nothing from that month used in training. If the training orders ran right up to January, their labels would still be open, and the model would be taught "no repeat" for customers who simply had not had time yet.

scikit-learn's TimeSeriesSplit names the rule: it "provides train/test indices to split time-ordered data, where other cross-validation methods are inappropriate, as they would lead to training on future data and evaluating on past data." A random split of Serai's orders would mix February customers into training and January customers into the test, and the score would describe a world where the future is known.

Self-studyWhy training AUC below test AUC is not good news2 min▶

Usually a model scores higher on the data it was trained on. Serai's does the opposite: 0.6248 in training, 0.7894 on month T+1. It is tempting to read that as "the model generalises brilliantly". The honest reading is duller. The training label is weak and noisy: 60 positives in 2,744 orders, many of them same-day repeats. The test month has 24 positives. With numbers that small, the two AUCs can land in either order by chance. Report both, and say out loud that the ordering is noise here.

What would make you worry is the other direction by a wide margin: a training AUC far above the next month's. Part 3 is that case.

Real world

The survey arrives after the parcel. Olist's documentation says its satisfaction survey goes out once the customer receives the product or the estimated delivery date is due. So a review score is not known at the moment of purchase, which is when Serai's model is asked to score. The seven features the model does use (prior orders, recency of the last one, order value, freight share, instalments, voucher use, items in the order) are all known when the order is placed. A feature that arrives after the decision belongs to the next model, not this one.

Part 3 · covers leakage and the model that looks perfect

A model that looks perfect

5 min live

The most dangerous model review is the one where the numbers are excellent. Add one innocent-looking warehouse column to Serai's model and training AUC jumps to 0.9913. On the next month the same model finds fewer repeat buyers than the honest one, and falls under the gate.

LiveWhat a leak is, in one sentence3 min▶

Kaufman, Rosset, Perlich and Stitelman define leakage as "the introduction of information about the data mining target that should not be legitimately available to mine from". The column has_later_order is the target wearing a different name: it is 1 for every training order that was followed by another, because a nightly reporting job filled it in after the fact. In training it answers the question for the model. At scoring, in February 2018, no later order has happened yet, so the column is 0 for every row and carries nothing.

The cost is worse than "the training number was inflated". With the answer on the table, the model learns to lean on that one column and lets the honest signals drift. When the column goes blank, what is left ranks worse than the model that never saw it: 4 of 24 repeats in the top 58 instead of 9, lift 1.65x against a 2.0x gate. Kaufman and colleagues propose a "learn-predict separation": every input stamped with the time it became known, and nothing used that was known only after the moment of prediction.

Self-studyThree questions for any model that looks too good2 min▶
  • Which month was it scored on? If the answer is "a random 20% of the rows", the score mixes past and future. Ask for the next unseen month.
  • When was each feature known? For every column, the time it becomes known against the time the model is asked. Any column nobody can date waits.
  • How far apart are training and next-month results? Serai's leaked model reads 0.9913 in training and 1.65x lift on the next month, under its gate. A gap like that is a leak until proven otherwise.

Combine the leak with skipped data engineering and month T+1 AUC falls to 0.4922, worse than ranking at random. Two shortcuts that each look survivable compound into a model that is worse than no model.

Real world

The column was right there in the warehouse. Fields like "has a later order", "lifetime orders" or "churned flag" are usually built for reporting dashboards, where hindsight is exactly what you want. The first person to build a model finds them already joined and well populated, and they make the model look superb. The leak is not carelessness with the algorithm; it is a reporting column crossing into a prediction table without anyone asking when its values became known.

Build-along

The worth sheet

★ 20 min · everyone builds

You need one model your organisation runs or is being pitched, and the person who built it. No model to hand? Fill the sheet for Serai with the numbers on this page. The cost and value lines are yours to supply: nobody can hand you those, and a sheet that invents them is worth less than a blank.

Write the decision first. One sentence: who acts on the model's output, what they do differently, and how often. If you cannot write it, stop here; the model has no worth yet, however good its scores.

★ Serai's decision lineDecision: each month, brand CRM teams contact the top tenth of last month's buyers Instead of: contacting everyone, or a random sample Owner: CRM lead per brand; model owner on Serai's data team

Get the base rate and the lift, on the next unseen month. Ask for the counts, not just the ratios, so you can do the small-number check yourself.

★ The counts behind the ratiosScoring month: February 2018 (month T+1), never used in training Orders scored: 574 Repeats among them: 24 Base rate: 24 / 574 = 4.18% Top decile: 58 orders Repeats in it: 9 Top-decile rate: 9 / 58 = 15.5% Lift: 15.5% / 4.18% = 3.71x One hit is worth: (1 / 58) / 4.18% = 0.41 of lift

Translate into repeats found per 100 contacts. This is the line a budget owner reads.

★ Per 100 contacts (arithmetic on the counts above)Contact at random: about 4.18 repeat buyers per 100 contacts Contact the top decile: about 15.5 repeat buyers per 100 contacts Difference: about 11.3 more per 100 contacts Share of all repeats reached from 10% of the list: 9 of 24 = 37.5%

Add the cost per contact and the value per repeat as YOUR assumptions. Write them as ranges, and label them as assumptions on the sheet. Then write the break-even in words.

★ Your assumptions, not oursCost per contact: USD ____ to ____ (assumption: message, voucher, staff time) Value per repeat order: USD ____ to ____ (assumption: margin, not GMV) Break-even: the top decile pays when 15.5 x value per repeat > 100 x cost per contact Caveat: the model finds people likely to repeat; it does not prove the contact caused the repeat. Only a held-out control group can measure that.

Check the gate and the leak questions, then write the verdict.

Yes, seven features; review score excluded Gap check: training vs next month 0.6248 vs 0.7894 AUC; ordering is noise Verdict: live, read as "roughly 3.5x to 4x on 24 positives"; re-check on a second month

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 3 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · In month T+1, 24 of 574 orders were followed by a repeat, and the model's top 58 held 9 of them. What does that tell a CRM lead?

Lift is the top decile's hit rate over the base rate: 15.5% against 4.18%, so 3.71x. It measures who the model ranks high, not what a contact causes (C), and AUC is a ranking score, not an accuracy (A).

2 · A new column lifts training AUC from 0.6248 to 0.9913. What should you ask first?

On Serai's model the column was has_later_order, filled in with hindsight. Next-month lift fell to 1.65x, under the 2.0x gate. A leap in training score is a leak until the time each value was known says otherwise.

3 · With the data engineering layer skipped, lift reads 3.30x instead of 3.71x. A team proposes choosing between two model versions on that kind of gap. What is the honest response?

3.71x and 3.30x are 9 and 8 hits in the top 58. A single repeat buyer separates them. Training AUC (D) describes memorisation, not next month.
