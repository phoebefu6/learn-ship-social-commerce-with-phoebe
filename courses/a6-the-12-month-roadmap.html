The 12-month roadmap

Most data roadmaps are a list of launches with dates beside them. Tonight's is a list of gates. Serai Commerce, the constructed enabler running 48 brands on 8,614 real orders, has five layers to build and twelve months to build them in. Each layer gets a quarter, a gate it must pass, and an owner who signs it off; nothing above a layer starts going live until that layer has passed. You leave with your own roadmap on one page, a list of things to stop doing, and the go/hold board you will take to every quarterly steering meeting.

Part 1 · covers the five layers sequenced over 12 months

Four quarters, five gates

6 min live

A 12-month plan for five layers looks like a timeline, but it reads like a chain. Each bar starts when the gate before it passes. Move one gate and every bar to its right moves with it, which is exactly the property a calendar of launch dates hides.

LiveEach quarter: what builds, what gate closes it, who signs4 min▶
Quarter (constructed)What buildsGate that closes itWhat it unlocks
Q1Data engineering: three exports into one merged schema, four checks per export, the repair rulesG1 · 0 blocking failures after repair (the raw exports start at 333)Every layer above. This is the gate all four others inherit.
Q2Analytics: the metric registry, orders, GMV and repeat rate, one definition eachG2 · registry agreement of at least 99.5% with a control totalThe model's labels and the facts gen AI quotes
Q3ML and gen AI side by side: the repeat-purchase model, listing copy and review summariesG3 · month T+1 lift of at least 2.0x · G4 · rubric pass of at least 70% and golden agreement of at least 85%Two independent shares of the product's price
Q4The data product: the seller pack, priced as a rangeG5 · the price counts only the layers that passed G1 to G4Revenue, as a range from stated assumptions

ML and gen AI share Q3 because neither consumes the other: both consume the registry. That is the one place in the chain where two teams can work in parallel without one waiting on the other. The quarter lengths themselves are a constructed plan for Serai; your own will be set by how long your bottom layer takes, and nobody else's benchmark can tell you that.

LiveA gate is reviewed on a date, by a name2 min▶

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework splits risk work into four functions, "govern, map, measure, and manage". A roadmap uses all four, in that order. Govern is the owner column: a named person who can say no. Map is the estate sheet from session 1: what each layer consumes and who depends on it. Measure is the gate's number, which the framework describes as using quantitative, qualitative or mixed methods to analyse, assess, benchmark and monitor risk. Manage is what happens on the review date: go, or hold.

Put the review date on the roadmap, not just the build date. "Registry live in Q2" is a wish. "Registry agreement reviewed on the last Friday of Q2 by the analytics lead; below 99.5% it holds and the model's start moves" is a plan.

Self-studyWhen the plan should be shorter than 12 months2 min▶

Twelve months is a budgeting cycle, not a law. If your data engineering layer already passes a gate like G1 today, start at Q2 and the plan shortens. If it does not and your exports are messier than Serai's, Q1 may need two quarters, and the honest roadmap shows the product in the following year rather than squeezing every bar to fit the calendar. A roadmap that keeps its end date by shrinking the bottom bar is a demo-first roadmap wearing a gate-first costume.

Real world

The bottom bar is the one that hides real surprises. Olist's public order rows are a cleaned, anonymised dataset, and they still hold 122 orders in Serai's sample whose carrier pickup is logged before payment approval, plus one with no approval time at all. Those are real, not constructed. Whoever estimates Q1 without opening the files first is estimating the defects they already know about, and a careful check always finds some they did not.

Part 2 · covers the gate each layer must pass before the next starts

What to stop doing

6 min live

A gate-first roadmap is mostly the same work as a demo-first one, in a different order. The difference is what each quarter is allowed to put in front of a seller. Reordering costs nothing to draw; the hard part is the list of habits it asks you to give up.

LiveFive things to stop doing, and why4 min▶
StopBecauseDo instead
Putting the data foundation last as a "stretch goal"Every layer above consumes it; on the bench, skipping it holds 65% of the product's priceMake it Q1 with G1 as the only exit
Promoting a demo to production on the rows it was demoed onOn raw rows, 7 of 20 gen AI outputs pass and the rubric agrees with the golden verdict only 50% of the timeKeep the demo; hold its launch until G1 and G2 pass
Reporting training AUC as the model's resultA backfilled field lifts training AUC to 0.9913 while month T+1 lift falls to 1.65x, under the gateReport month T+1 lift against the base rate, every quarter
Putting a conversion uplift in the business case for AI copyThe evidence disagrees (session 4): randomized platform experiments and survey experiments answer different questionsPrice the copy layer on its rubric and golden set; measure uplift on your own catalogue later
Quoting the product's value as one numberEvery input is an assumption; Serai's first-year net runs from -USD 235,200 to USD 512,400Show the range and the assumption that decides its sign (session 5)
LiveWhat a gate-first roadmap does not stop2 min▶

It does not stop demos. A demo built on a sample in Q1 is often how a data team earns the budget for Q1 at all. What it stops is the demo going in front of a paying seller before the rows under it pass G1 and G2. Keep the demo in the steering pack, label it "demo, not live", and let the board say when it becomes live.

It also does not stop parallel work. Data engineers in Q1 and the analytics team drafting registry definitions in Q1 are not in conflict; the registry just cannot be declared live until G1 passes. The gate controls the live date, not the start of thinking.

Real world

One order, several sellers, one export row each. Olist describes its orders as possibly holding several items, each fulfilled by a different seller. A copy assistant shipped in Q1 on per-seller extracts sees each seller's slice; the merged order, and the review score attached to it, exist only after the Q1 data engineering work joins them. That is why a demo-first Q1 is cheap to show and expensive to keep: its facts come from a grain the bottom layer has not fixed yet.

Part 3 · covers the board page with the five-layer scorecard

The go/hold board

5 min live

Every quarter ends at the same page: one row per layer, the measured number, its gate, and a single word, live or held. The board is the scorecard from session 1 put on a review cadence. Here it is in three states the bench can print, so you have seen what each failure looks like before it shows up on your own.

LiveReading the board in a steering meeting3 min▶

Read it bottom up, the same way the layers are built. If the DE row is held, stop reading: every row above it is measured on rows that have not passed, and a live word higher up means only that the noise happened to land kindly. The middle column shows exactly that. With data engineering skipped the model still reads 3.30x and clears its gate, but it clears it on 108 labelled "repeats" where the repaired frame holds 60. A live model on a held foundation is not a result to celebrate; it is a row to re-measure once G1 passes.

The right column is the opposite failure. The foundation is fine and so is the registry; the model alone is held because a field filled in with hindsight lifted training AUC to 0.9913 while month T+1 lift fell to 1.65x. The fix lives in one team, and the board shows that the other layers keep 65% of the price while it is fixed.

LiveThree rules for the board page2 min▶
  • Print the measured number beside the word. "Held" without "65.14%" invites a debate about feelings; with it, the debate is about a threshold someone owns.
  • Print the range, not the midpoint. The net range changes width and sign with the live share. A board that prints one number hides the column that matters.
  • Say how small the numbers are. Month T+1 holds 24 real repeat buyers, so one hit moves lift by 0.41. A lift of 2.1x against a 2.0x gate is a pass, and also one customer away from a hold. Write that next to it.
Self-studyThe fourth state, and why it belongs in the pack2 min▶

The bench can also print both failures at once: data engineering skipped and the field leaked. Month T+1 AUC falls to 0.4922, lift to 1.24x, nothing is live, the price share is 0% and the first-year net runs from -USD 307,200 to -USD 159,600: a range that never touches zero. Put it in the appendix of the first steering pack. It is the cheapest way to show a sponsor that skipping a layer and trusting a training score are not two small risks but one large one.

Real world

A review summary that is right about the wrong rows. On the repaired frame, Store 2 has 153 reviews with a mean score of 3.9 and 18% at one or two stars. On the raw exports the same store reads 155. A summary that quotes 153 is correct, and the rubric fails it anyway because the facts it checks against were recomputed from rows holding re-sends. That is why the gen AI row on the board turns held when the DE row does, even though nothing about the copy changed: the board is only as honest as the bottom row.

Homework

Before your next steering meeting

30 min
  1. Finish your one-page roadmap: four quarters, five gates, a review date and an owner for each, and what is held if each gate is missed.
  2. Take your current roadmap and mark every item with the gate it silently assumes has passed. Count how many items are dated before that gate's review.
  3. Ask the owner of your bottom layer for today's blocking-failure count across your source systems. Write it on the board's DE row, even if the answer is "we do not measure it yet".
  4. Pick one item for your stop list and say it, with its reason, to the person who funds the roadmap. Note what they ask back; that question is your next gate.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Serai's registry misses G2 at the end of Q2. What happens to the roadmap?

The model's labels and the facts gen AI quotes both come from the registry, so a missed G2 moves every bar to its right. D is the trap: the registry's share is held, but so is the start of the two layers that consume it.

2 · On the board with data engineering skipped, the model reads lift 3.30x and clears its 2.0x gate. How should a leader read that row?

The board is read bottom up. With the DE row held, a live word higher up means the noise landed kindly: the model trained on 108 "repeats" where the repaired frame holds 60. Meanwhile the registry and gen AI rows are held and 65% of the price with them.

3 · A sponsor wants the business case for the copy layer to include "AI-written listings lift conversion". What goes on the roadmap instead?

Session 4's disagreement stands: randomized platform experiments found effects from no detectable impact to 16.3% depending on the workflow, while survey experiments (reported) were about mentioning AI, a different question. Neither is a forecast for Serai, and averaging them mixes two different outcomes.
