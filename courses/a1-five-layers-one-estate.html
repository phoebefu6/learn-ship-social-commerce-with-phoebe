Five layers, one estate

Every social-commerce data roadmap gets pitched from the top: a copy assistant, a churn model, a dashboard for brands. Tonight starts from the bottom. Serai Commerce, a constructed Southeast Asian enabler running 48 brands across three marketplaces, receives one file per marketplace. Its 8,614 real orders arrive as 8,766 rows. Five layers sit between those files and anything Serai could sell, and each layer only works if the one below it does. You leave with the five layers drawn for your own estate and the scorecard you would sign.

Part 1 · covers the five layers and what each consumes

Each layer eats the one below

6 min live

A data estate is not a list of projects. It is a stack in which every layer takes something from the layer below and turns it into something the layer above can trust. Name what each layer consumes and the build order stops being a matter of taste.

LiveThe five layers, defined by what they consume and produce4 min▶
LayerConsumesProducesThe test that proves it
1 · Data engineeringThree marketplace exports, each in its own shapeOne merged schema: orders, lines, payments, review scores, timestampsKeys unique, customer keys resolve, timestamps in order, amounts not negative
2 · AnalyticsOne row per real orderA metric registry: one definition per metric, computed one wayThe registry reproduces a control total
3 · Machine learningRegistry-defined history with honest labelsA ranked list of customers likely to order againLift on the next month, never on the training months
4 · Gen AITrue facts about listings and storesDraft listing copy and review summariesA rubric, checked against a hand-labelled golden set
5 · Data productEverything above, each with its gateA pack sellers pay forA revenue and cost range from stated assumptions

The analytics, model and gen AI layers all consume the same merged rows, so the data engineering layer is the one every other layer inherits. Sessions 2 to 6 take the layers one at a time; the builder track builds each of them for real, and Builder session 10 puts all five on one bench.

LiveWhy "start with the AI" fails on this stack3 min▶

A copy assistant is the most visible thing on the stack and the cheapest demo. It is also the layer with the most dependencies. A review summary that says "153 reviews, average 3.9" is only right if the rows under it were de-duplicated and the stores resolved. When the rows are wrong the summary is fluent and wrong, which NIST's Generative AI Profile files under confabulation: "the production of confidently stated but erroneous or false content". Session 4 shows this happening on Serai's own summaries.

The demo-first roadmap is not stupid; it buys attention and budget. It becomes expensive when the demo is promoted to production on the same rows it was demoed on. Fund the demo, then fund the layers it needs before it goes in front of a seller.

Real world

Real orders under a constructed company. Olist, a Brazilian marketplace integrator, published around 100,000 orders from 2016 to 2018 made at multiple marketplaces in Brazil, described as "real commercial data" that "has been anonymised", under a non-commercial share-alike licence (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). One order can hold several items, each fulfilled by a different seller. Serai's 8,614 orders are a seeded sample of the delivered ones, the same sample the Enablers course uses, so the numbers on both courses line up. The company, its three exports and their defects are constructed; the orders, prices, freight, timestamps and review scores are real.

Part 2 · covers what raw exports look like before any check

What arrives is not what happened

6 min live

Add up the three files as they land and every headline number is slightly wrong, each in its own direction. Nothing looks broken. That is the problem the bottom layer exists to solve.

LiveThe four defects in Serai's exports, and which are real4 min▶
DefectCount on the benchReal or constructedWhat it does to the numbers
An order re-sent under a variant key152Constructed (1%, 2% and 4% by export)Inflates orders and GMV; turns one-time buyers into "repeat" buyers
A customer key missing from the customer table113Constructed (0%, 1% and 5% by export)Splits a returning customer into a stranger
A refund posted as a negative line64Constructed (Export B only)Nets money out of a gross metric
Carrier pickup logged before payment approval122, plus 1 order with no approval timeReal, in the Olist rowsChanges no metric, but breaks any delivery-time feature built on it

The constructed rates are modest on purpose. Export C, the smallest marketplace, is the dirtiest, which is the usual pattern: the newest channel gets the least integration work. The real timestamp inversions are a useful reminder that a public, cleaned dataset still carries rows a careful check flags.

Self-studyWhy the naive repeat rate is low while the dirty registry is high3 min▶

The naive sum counts customers inside each export, so a person who bought once on two marketplaces looks like two one-time buyers: 2.90% against the true 3.00%. Session 2 shows the opposite error. Run the registry's definition on the raw rows and every re-sent order makes its customer look like a repeat buyer: the repeat rate reads 4.61%. Two wrong numbers, on either side of the right one, from the same files. Which one your team reports depends on who wrote the query.

Part 3 · covers the five-layer scorecard and its gates

The scorecard a leader signs

5 min live

A leader does not need every metric on the bench. She needs one row per layer: what was measured, the gate it had to clear, and whether that layer is live. The same board shows what happens when a layer is skipped.

LiveGates are a policy, not a standard3 min▶

Serai's constructed gates: no blocking data-quality failure after repair; registry agreement of at least 99.5% with a control total; a month T+1 lift of at least 2.0 times the base rate; gen AI that passes its rubric on at least 70% of outputs while the rubric agrees with a human golden verdict at least 85% of the time. None of these thresholds comes from a standard. They are the numbers Serai's leadership agreed it could defend to a paying seller, and your own will differ.

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework splits risk work into four functions, "govern, map, measure, and manage". A gate is where "measure" meets "manage": a measured number, a threshold someone owns, and a consequence when it is missed. A threshold without a consequence is a dashboard.

LiveRanges, not points, for the product row2 min▶

The product row prints a range because every input is an assumption: 40 to 160 paying sellers, USD 150 to 350 a seller a month, a build of USD 150,000 to 240,000 and a run cost of USD 20 to 35 a seller a month. With all four layers live the first-year net runs from minus USD 235,200 to USD 512,400. A range that crosses zero is not a failure of analysis. It tells you which assumption to go and test before anyone signs, and session 5 works out which one.

Real world

The model survived the dirty data; the registry did not. On the bench, skipping the data engineering layer moves the model's lift from 3.71x to 3.30x: its top 58 now hold 8 of the month's 24 real repeat buyers instead of 9. It moves the repeat rate from 3.00% to 4.61%. That is the honest nuance: re-sent rows mostly add noise to a ranking, but they rewrite a count. The layers that print numbers to a seller take the damage first.

Build-along

Your estate on one sheet

★ 20 min · everyone builds

You need a list of the data and AI work your organisation is funding or being pitched, and ten minutes with whoever owns your order data. No list to hand? Do it for Serai with the numbers on this page. Your sheet will look different, which is the point.

List every data and AI initiative on the table. Funded, pitched or rumoured. Write each on its own line with the team that wants it.

Put each one in a layer. A dashboard is analytics. A churn score is ML. A copy assistant is gen AI. A "brand insight pack" is a product. A pipeline or a schema change is data engineering. Anything that spans two layers is two items.

★ Serai's list, layered5 product Seller growth pack (registry numbers + repeat list + copy assistant) 4 gen AI Listing copy drafts; review summaries per store 3 ML Repeat-purchase list for brand CRM teams 2 analytics Orders, GMV, repeat rate for the monthly brand review 1 DE Merge three marketplace exports into one schema, with tests

For each layer, write what it consumes from the layer below. Use the words in the figure: rows, labels, facts, numbers. If you cannot say what a layer consumes, it is floating, and floating layers are where demos live.

Write the test and the gate. One measurable check per layer and the threshold that makes it live. Borrow Serai's gates if you have nothing yet, and mark them as borrowed.

Name an owner per row and write the sentence. Read it to whoever funds the roadmap.

★ The steering sentenceWe fund the five layers in order. Layer N goes live only when layer N-1 passes its gate: DE has zero blocking failures, the registry matches its control total, the model beats the base rate on a month it never saw, and the gen AI rubric agrees with a human golden set. The product's price counts only live layers.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 1 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Serai's three exports hold 8,766 order rows for 8,614 real orders. Which layer exists to close that gap?

The re-sends carry a variant key, so a registry counting distinct keys still counts them; only a check that normalises keys finds them. The model and the summaries consume the same rows and inherit the error.

2 · With the data engineering layer skipped, the model's lift moves from 3.71x to 3.30x but the repeat rate moves from 3.00% to 4.61%. What is the honest reading?

The model lost one hit in 24 real repeats; the registry's repeat rate is off by more than half. B is the trap: a skipped layer still holds back the registry and gen AI rows, 65% of the product's price on Serai's assumptions.

3 · Serai's product row prints a first-year net from minus USD 235,200 to USD 512,400. What should a leader do with a range that crosses zero?

Every input is an assumption, so the range is honest and the midpoint is not a forecast. The useful move is to find the assumption that decides the sign; session 5 does it.
