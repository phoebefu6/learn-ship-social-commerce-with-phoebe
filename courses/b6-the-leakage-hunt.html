Part 0

A model that looks perfect is a question, not an answer

Serai Commerce is the constructed enabler from the Enablers track; its 8,614 orders are real derived Olist rows. Session 5 trained a logistic regression on seven features known at purchase, with a label window that closes on 2018-01-31 and a scoring month of February 2018. Month T+1 has 574 orders and 24 real repeats, a base rate of 4.18%, and the model puts 9 of them in its top 58: lift 3.71x. Tonight's job is the opposite of tuning. You add a column, watch the numbers move the wrong way, and learn to ask one question of every feature before it is allowed near a fit: when was this value known? Model depth beyond the leak, calibration, tuning and monitoring, belongs to the ML production, model evaluation and feature engineering tracks; this session ships one model and hunts its leak.

Part 1 · covers the backfilled field and what it does to the numbers

A field from the future

6 min live

The column is has_later_order: does this customer have any order after this one? A nightly reporting job fills it for every historical row, looking back from today. For a training order whose customer came back, it reads 1. For an order being scored in February 2018, the next order has not happened yet, so it reads 0 for all 574 rows. Training sees the answer; scoring does not.

Read the first column as a warning, not a score. A training AUC of 0.9913 on a label as noisy as "another order within 90 days" is not a model getting good; it is a model that found a column that already contains the label. The honest model's training AUC sits below its month T+1 AUC, which on 24 positives is small-sample noise, not a sign of quality. With 24 positives in month T+1, one hit moves lift by 0.41, so treat 1.65x against 3.71x as "five fewer real repeats in the top decile", not as a precise ratio.

LiveWhere the weight goes2 min▶

The session 10 widget prints the weights on the standardised features. With the field present, gradient descent puts 1.389 on has_later_order and at most 0.255 on any other feature. The honest features drift, because once the answer column is on the table they barely reduce the loss. At scoring the leaked column is a constant 0, so it adds nothing to the ranking, and what is left is the drifted weights on the seven honest features. That is why the leaked model ranks February worse than the honest one: 4 real repeats in the top 58 instead of 9.

Switch on the session 2 anti-lever as well, so the model also trains on raw exports with re-sent orders, and month T+1 AUC falls to 0.4922. Below 0.5 means the ranking is no better than a random draw on this month, and on 24 positives it is probably not meaningfully worse either. Both levers together take the model's 35% share of the product price to zero, which session 9 prices.

Real world

Every public dataset is a snapshot taken after the fact. The Olist file covers orders from 2016 to 2018 and was published once, whole. Any column you compute across the full file, such as "this person has another order later" through customer_unique_id, is computed from the end of 2018 looking back. In a notebook it looks like an ordinary groupby. In production the same groupby runs on the day of scoring and returns 0 for everyone who has not come back yet. A warehouse is the same kind of snapshot, refreshed nightly.

Part 2 · covers leakage as defined in the literature and learn-predict separation

Learn-predict separation

6 min live

Kaufman, Rosset, Perlich and Stitelman (2012) define leakage as "the introduction of information about the data mining target that should not be legitimately available to mine from", and propose a "learn-predict separation". Turned into a working rule: stamp every column with the time its value became known, draw a line at the moment of prediction, and build each training row only from what sits to the left of that line.

LiveWhy a time split cannot save you from a column3 min▶

Session 5's split already obeys the scikit-learn warning that shuffled cross-validation on time-ordered data would lead "to training on future data and evaluating on past data". Every training ROW is older than every scoring row. Leakage lives one level down, inside a COLUMN: has_later_order on a 2017 training row was computed in 2018. A clean split of rows does nothing about a column whose values were filled from the future. You need both: rows split by time, and columns stamped by time.

The separation is about information, not about whether a column "is the label". has_later_order is not the label (it ignores the 90-day window), yet it carries most of the label's information. Kaufman et al. are explicit that leakage is information about the target that should not be legitimately available, which covers proxies, not just copies.

Self-studyWhat the paper offers, and what is quoted here2 min▶

Only the abstract of Kaufman, Rosset, Perlich and Stitelman (2012) has been read at source, so only the abstract is quoted on this page: the definition, the learn-predict separation, and the paper's aim of detecting leakage when the modeler cannot control how the data was collected. That last case is Serai's: the backfill job belongs to the reporting team, not to the person building the model. Anything beyond the abstract, such as specific detection procedures, is not claimed here; read the paper itself before citing its methods.

Real world

The documentation that states the row rule. scikit-learn's TimeSeriesSplit exists because "other cross-validation methods are inappropriate" for time-ordered data, and its successive training sets are supersets of those that come before them. That is the row half of learn-predict separation, built into a library. No library ships the column half: knowing when each value became known is metadata only your team can write down, which is why the build-along below writes it as a table.

Part 3 · covers review score as a timing trap and a checklist for late fields

Timing traps

5 min live

Not every leak is a backfill job. Some columns are honest facts about an order that simply arrive after the moment you need them. Review score is the classic one in commerce: it predicts loyalty, it sits on the same order, and it does not exist yet at purchase.

LiveWhy review score stays out of the repeat model3 min▶

The Olist dataset notes say the satisfaction survey is sent once the customer receives the product, or when the estimated delivery date is due. So at the moment of purchase, the only moment Serai's model is asked about, there is no review score for that order. In Serai's sample, 197 orders were followed by another order from the same customer within 90 days, and 83 of those repeats were placed on the same day as the order before them: before a carrier pickup, a delivery or a survey existed. A model trained with review score would be scored, in production, on a column that is always missing at the moment it matters.

The same-day repeats carry their own ambiguity, which session 4 treats: some are split baskets, some real second purchases. For leakage the answer does not matter. Either way the second order happened before the first order's review could exist, so review score cannot have caused it and cannot be used to predict it at purchase.

LiveA checklist for spotting fields known only after the label2 min▶
  • Who writes it, and when? A column filled by a nightly or monthly job, rather than at the event, was written later than the row it sits on. Find the job and its schedule.
  • Does its name look forward? "Later", "lifetime", "total to date", "churned", "returned", "final status", "has ever": each one describes a period that extends past the moment of prediction.
  • Does it come from a downstream event? Delivery, review, refund, support ticket, chargeback: if the event follows the purchase, so does every column it feeds.
  • Can it be updated after the row is created? A mutable column holds its latest value, not the value it had on the day. Keep the change history or treat it as late.
  • Is it suspiciously good? A single feature that pushes training AUC toward 1 on a noisy label is a leak until proven otherwise. Fit with and without it on a time split and compare next-month lift, not training AUC.
  • Is it missing exactly where the future has not happened? A column that is mostly blank or 0 on the newest rows, and full on the old ones, is being filled with hindsight.
Self-studyWhen the timestamps themselves disagree2 min▶

A time-known stamp is only as good as the clock behind it. In Serai's sample, 122 real Olist orders log a carrier pickup before the payment approval, and 1 has no approval timestamp at all. Session 2 flags those rows and keeps them, because they do not change any metric. For a leak audit they are a reminder: if you stamp a feature with "known at approval", check that approval is a reliable event in your data before trusting the order of events. When two clocks disagree, stamp the feature with the later of the two.

Real world

One order, several reviews, several answers. In Serai's sample, 39 orders carry more than one review and 68 carry none. A review score column at the order grain is therefore a choice (first by answer time, in session 1's fact table) made after every review is in. Even a model that waited until delivery would see a value picked with hindsight about which reviews would arrive. Late facts tend to come with late choices attached.

Build-along

A leak audit table for a feature store

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Four blocks in pandas and scikit-learn. Blocks 1 and 2 need no data and their printed output is shown as it ran; the audit rows are constructed for Serai's model. Blocks 3 and 4 run on the public Olist file, downloaded with kagglehub.dataset_download("olistbr/brazilian-ecommerce"); they print nothing here because your numbers will differ from Serai's 8,614-order sample.

Write the audit as data. One row per feature: where it comes from, the event at which its value becomes known, and the event at which the model needs it. Events are ordered, so "known after needed" is a comparison, not a judgement call.

★ Block 1 · the audit table (constructed rows, output as run)import pandas as pd # the order in which events happen in one customer's life EVENT_ORDER = {"purchase": 0, "approval": 1, "delivery": 2, "review": 3, "next order": 4} audit = pd.DataFrame([ ("prior_orders_log", "orders", "purchase", "purchase"), ("recency_prior", "orders", "purchase", "purchase"), ("order_value_log", "lines", "purchase", "purchase"), ("freight_share", "lines", "purchase", "purchase"), ("installments", "payments", "purchase", "purchase"), ("voucher_used", "payments", "purchase", "purchase"), ("items_in_order", "lines", "purchase", "purchase"), ("review_score", "review_scores", "review", "purchase"), ("has_later_order", "nightly backfill", "next order", "purchase"), ], columns=["feature", "source", "time_known", "time_needed"]) late = audit.time_known.map(EVENT_ORDER) > audit.time_needed.map(EVENT_ORDER) audit["verdict"] = late.map({False: "keep", True: "LEAK"}) print(audit[["feature", "time_known", "time_needed", "verdict"]].to_string(index=False)) print("pass", (~late).sum(), "of", len(audit), "| leaks:", list(audit.feature[late])) # prints: # feature time_known time_needed verdict # prior_orders_log purchase purchase keep # recency_prior purchase purchase keep # order_value_log purchase purchase keep # freight_share purchase purchase keep # installments purchase purchase keep # voucher_used purchase purchase keep # items_in_order purchase purchase keep # review_score review purchase LEAK # has_later_order next order purchase LEAK # pass 7 of 9 | leaks: ['review_score', 'has_later_order']

Turn the table into a gate that fails loudly. Two rules, the same spirit as session 2's tests: a column nobody has audited does not get in, and a column known too late does not get in. Call it at the top of every training script.

★ Block 2 · the leak gate (output as run)def leak_gate(columns, audit): row = audit.set_index("feature") unaudited = [c for c in columns if c not in row.index] too_late = [c for c in columns if c in row.index and row.verdict[c] == "LEAK"] if unaudited or too_late: raise ValueError(f"leak gate: unaudited {unaudited}, known too late {too_late}") try: leak_gate(["prior_orders_log", "installments", "review_score", "days_to_ship"], audit) except ValueError as e: print(e) # prints: leak gate: unaudited ['days_to_ship'], known too late ['review_score']

Check one timing claim on real rows. On the full public file, count the repeat orders within 90 days, how many were placed on the same calendar day, and how many were placed before the earlier order's first review was answered. Only the review timestamp is read, never the text.

★ Block 3 · the review-score timing trap on the public fileimport numpy as np d = "path/to/olist/" o = pd.read_csv(d + "olist_orders_dataset.csv", parse_dates=["order_purchase_timestamp"]) c = pd.read_csv(d + "olist_customers_dataset.csv") r = pd.read_csv(d + "olist_order_reviews_dataset.csv", usecols=["order_id", "review_answer_timestamp"], parse_dates=["review_answer_timestamp"]) o = o[o.order_status == "delivered"].merge(c[["customer_id", "customer_unique_id"]], on="customer_id") o = o.sort_values(["customer_unique_id", "order_purchase_timestamp"]) o["next_purchase"] = o.groupby("customer_unique_id").order_purchase_timestamp.shift(-1) first_answer = r.sort_values("review_answer_timestamp").groupby("order_id").review_answer_timestamp.first() o = o.join(first_answer, on="order_id") gap = o.next_purchase - o.order_purchase_timestamp rep = o[gap <= pd.Timedelta(days=90)] print("repeats within 90 days", len(rep)) print("same calendar day", (rep.next_purchase.dt.date == rep.order_purchase_timestamp.dt.date).sum()) print("placed before the earlier review was answered", (rep.next_purchase < rep.review_answer_timestamp).sum()) # your numbers will differ from Serai's sample (197 repeats, 83 same-day): the full file is larger

Prove a suspect with a with-and-without fit. Build a few honest features as of purchase, add the backfilled column, split by time as in session 5, and set the column to 0 on the scoring month, because that is what it reads at scoring. Compare month T+1 lift, not training AUC.

★ Block 4 · the leak, measured (scikit-learn)from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score items = pd.read_csv(d + "olist_order_items_dataset.csv") pays = pd.read_csv(d + "olist_order_payments_dataset.csv") lines = items.groupby("order_id").agg(price=("price", "sum"), freight=("freight_value", "sum"), n_items=("order_item_id", "size")) inst = pays.groupby("order_id").payment_installments.max().rename("installments") o = o.join(lines, on="order_id").join(inst, on="order_id").dropna(subset=["price", "installments"]) o["prior_orders"] = np.log1p(o.groupby("customer_unique_id").cumcount()) o["value_log"] = np.log1p(o.price) o["freight_share"] = o.freight / (o.price + o.freight) o["label"] = (gap.reindex(o.index) <= pd.Timedelta(days=90)).astype(int) o["has_later_order"] = o.next_purchase.notna().astype(int) # the backfill: hindsight t = o.order_purchase_timestamp train = o[t <= "2017-11-02"].copy() test = o[(t >= "2018-02-01") & (t < "2018-03-01")].copy() test["has_later_order"] = 0 # at scoring, no later order exists yet def top_decile_lift(y, p): k = max(1, len(y) // 10) top = np.argsort(-p)[:k] return y.values[top].mean() / y.mean() honest = ["prior_orders", "value_log", "freight_share", "installments", "n_items"] for name, cols in [("honest", honest), ("with the leak", honest + ["has_later_order"])]: m = LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000).fit(train[cols], train.label) p_tr = m.predict_proba(train[cols])[:, 1] p_te = m.predict_proba(test[cols])[:, 1] print(f"{name:14s} train AUC {roc_auc_score(train.label, p_tr):.4f} month T+1 AUC {roc_auc_score(test.label, p_te):.4f} lift {top_decile_lift(test.label, p_te):.2f}x") # your numbers will differ from the session 10 widget's 0.9913 and 1.65x: # different sample, five features instead of seven, a different solver

Register the audit beside the feature table. The audit is metadata, so store it where the features live and review it like code. A new column without an audit row fails block 2 by design: the cheapest moment to ask "when is this known?" is before anyone has seen how predictive it is.

Three questions

Clear all three and Builder session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Adding has_later_order lifts training AUC to 0.9913 and drops month T+1 lift to 1.65x. What happened?

The rows are split cleanly by time; the problem is inside a column. Training learns to lean on it (weight 1.389 against at most 0.255 elsewhere), and at scoring it is a constant, leaving drifted weights to rank the month: 4 real repeats in the top 58 instead of 9.

2 · Which rule is the learn-predict separation of Kaufman et al., put to work?

Leakage is information about the target that should not be legitimately available to mine from. Stamping each column with when it became known, and drawing the line at the moment of prediction, is that separation as a working rule. Shuffling time-ordered rows does the opposite.

3 · Review score clearly relates to loyalty. Why is it left out of Serai's repeat model?

The model is asked at purchase. Olist's survey is sent once the product arrives or the estimated date is due, so the column is always missing at that moment. Scores are licensed for use here; only the review text stays out. 39 is the count of orders with more than one review.
