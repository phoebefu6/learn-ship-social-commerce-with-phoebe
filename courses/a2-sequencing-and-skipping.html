Ship It: Social Commerce, End to End · Leader session 2 of 6

Sequencing and the cost of skipping

Every roadmap meeting has someone who wants to start with the exciting layer and "clean the data later". Tonight puts a price on later. Serai Commerce, the constructed enabler from session 1, runs its whole estate twice on the same 8,614 real orders: once built in order, once with the bottom layer skipped. The model hardly notices. The repeat rate jumps from 3.00% to 4.61%, good review summaries start failing, and 65% of what the data product can charge is held. You leave with a sequencing sheet: each layer, its gate, the earliest quarter it can go live, and what is held if it slips.

Part 0

From a stack to a schedule

Session 1 drew the five layers and named what each one consumes. Tonight turns that stack into an order of work. The rule is short: fund layer N only when layer N minus one passes its gate. Then we break the rule on purpose and follow the damage upward through Serai's registry, model and gen AI, and finally into the product's price. Serai Commerce is constructed for these pages, continued from the Social Commerce Enablers course; its orders are real, 8,614 delivered orders from the public Olist dataset, derived and masked. Part 1 · covers why DE, analytics, ML, gen AI, product in that order

Build order is a dependency, not a preference

6 min live

A build order argued from taste ("AI first, it is what the board wants") loses to whoever argues loudest next quarter. A build order argued from dependencies survives, because each step names the thing it needs and the test that proves it has it.

Read the second column carefully and one link is weaker than the others: gen AI does not consume the model's output. Its real dependencies are the clean rows and the registry's facts. Serai still places the model third for a practical reason: the model is judged by one number on a month it never saw, which builds the evaluation habit that gen AI then needs at a larger scale (a rubric and a golden set, session 4). A team that already runs evaluations well can run layers 3 and 4 side by side once the registry passes. What no team can do is move either of them below layer 2.

LiveThe demo-first roadmap, and when it is fine2 min▶

A demo-first roadmap builds the copy assistant on a sample, shows it to the board and earns the budget. That is a legitimate move, and often the only way a data team gets funded at all. It goes wrong at one specific moment: when the demo is promoted to production on the same unchecked rows it was demoed on.

So the honest version is two sentences. Demo on anything you like, label it a demo, and keep it away from sellers. Production starts at layer 1, and the demo's layer goes live only when every gate below it has passed. A demo with a written expiry date is a pitch; a demo without one becomes the product by default.

Self-studyWhy "parallel teams" do not remove the order3 min▶

Large organisations often answer the sequencing question with headcount: one team per layer, all starting on day one. That can be the right call for speed, and it changes nothing about the dependency. The model team can write features on raw rows, but its labels will count re-sends as repeats until the data team's repair lands. The gen AI team can write prompts, but its rubric checks facts that the registry has not yet fixed. Parallel teams shorten the calendar; they do not change which layer's gate decides when the others may ship. Write the gates first, then staff in parallel.

Real world

Even a cleaned public dataset needs layer 1. Olist's published orders are described as real commercial data, anonymised and released for reuse. Inside Serai's 8,614 of them sit 122 orders whose carrier pickup is logged before payment approval and 1 order with no approval time at all. Nobody constructed those; they came with the file. They change no headline metric, but any delivery-time feature or "days to ship" promise built on them would be wrong. A data engineering layer flags them and keeps them, which is a decision someone has to own before any layer above reads the timestamps.

Part 2 · covers the skip-DE cascade, layer by layer

What skipping DE costs, layer by layer

6 min live

Skipping the bottom layer does not break one thing. It hands the same raw rows to every layer above, and each layer turns them into a different kind of wrong. Follow one constructed defect, an order re-sent under a variant key, through all three.

LiveLayer by layer: what the re-send does3 min▶
LayerBuilt in orderDE skippedMechanism
Analytics8,614 orders; repeat rate 3.00% (250 of 8,333)8,766 orders; repeat rate 4.61% (384 of 8,337); agreement on repeat 65.14%A re-sent order gives a one-time buyer a second order, so the buyer becomes a "repeat customer"
MLTrain 2,744 orders, 60 positives; month T+1 lift 3.71xTrain 2,795 rows, 108 "positives"; month T+1 lift 3.30xThe label is "another order within 90 days"; a duplicate is another order on the same day
Gen AI15 of 20 pass; rubric agrees with golden 90%7 of 20 pass; agreement 50%; 9 false failsFacts are recomputed from rows: Store 2 reads 155 reviews, so a correct "153" summary fails

Notice the gen AI line. The 20 listing drafts and summaries are pre-written and constructed; nothing about the text changed. The facts they are checked against changed, so a correct sentence now looks wrong. A layer can fail with no change to its own work.

LiveHonest nuance: the model survived2 min▶

It would make a tidier story if skipping data engineering wrecked the model. It did not. Month T+1 lift moved from 3.71x to 3.30x, which is the model's top 58 orders holding 8 of the month's 24 real repeat buyers instead of 9. With only 24 positives, one hit moves lift by 0.41, so this difference is a single customer. Training AUC moved from 0.6248 to 0.6075 and month T+1 AUC from 0.7894 to 0.7707.

The reason is the shape of the damage. Re-sends mostly add noise to a ranking: a few extra "positives" in training blur the pattern without reversing it. A count has no such slack. Every re-send lands directly in the numerator of the repeat rate. So the honest leadership sentence is not "dirty data breaks AI". It is "dirty data breaks the layers that print numbers, and a model that survives it can still be sold beside a registry that cannot".

Self-studyIn production the gen AI failure runs the other way3 min▶

On the bench the summaries were written from the true facts and then checked against dirty ones, so they fail loudly: 9 false fails at once. A real pipeline with DE skipped is worse. The model writes from the dirty facts, the rubric checks against the same dirty facts, and every summary passes. "155 reviews" goes to the seller with a green tick.

NIST's Generative AI Profile calls the output side of this confabulation: "the production of confidently stated but erroneous or false content". The rubric cannot catch it, because a rubric is only as good as the facts it checks against. That is the strongest argument for layer 1 a gen AI sponsor will ever hear: without it, the evaluation itself is measuring agreement with the error.

Real world

Why a re-send is so easy to believe. Real customers in the Olist rows do place a second order on the same day. In Serai's sample, 197 orders are followed by another order from the same customer within 90 days, and 83 of those follow-ups are same-day. Some are split baskets, some are genuine second thoughts; the data cannot always tell. A constructed re-send looks exactly like that real pattern, which is why the model's "positives" nearly doubled without anyone noticing, and why only a keys-unique check with a normalised key finds them.

Part 3 · covers the live price share and the net range with DE skipped

Pricing the skip

5 min live

A steering committee hears "data quality" as a cost. Translate the skip into the product's price and it becomes a revenue decision. Serai prices its data product so that each layer's share counts only when its gate has passed.

LiveWhere the two ranges come from3 min▶

Every input is an assumption Serai wrote down, not a market fact: 40 to 160 paying sellers, USD 150 to 350 a seller a month, a build of USD 150,000 to 240,000 and a run cost of USD 20 to 35 a seller a month. The price splits into registry 40%, model 35% and gen AI 25%, and a share counts only when its layer's gate passes.

Live shareFirst-year revenueFirst-year costNet
Every layer built100%USD 72,000 to 672,000USD 159,600 to 307,200-USD 235,200 to USD 512,400
DE skipped35% (model only)USD 25,200 to 235,200 (35% of the row above, by arithmetic)USD 159,600 to 307,200-USD 282,000 to USD 75,600

The cost row is identical because the build and run costs do not care which gates passed. That is the line to read out in the meeting: skipping data engineering did not save its cost, it gave away most of the upside and kept every dollar of spend.

LiveA price share is a gate report2 min▶

Tying price to gates turns an engineering status into a commercial one. The registry share is held because agreement is 65.14% against a 99.5% gate. The gen AI share is held because the pass rate is 35% against 70% and agreement 50% against 85%. The model share stays live because 3.30x clears 2.0x. Nobody has to argue about whether the data is "good enough"; the gate argued it in advance.

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework names four functions, "govern, map, measure, and manage". A held share is "measure" producing a number and "manage" acting on it without a meeting. The thresholds themselves are Serai's constructed policy, not a standard; your own will differ, and they should be written down before the first number comes in, not after.

Self-studyThe other way to lose share: a leak2 min▶

Skipping a layer is one way to switch shares off. Building a layer badly is another. The bench's second break button adds a field known only in hindsight to the model: training AUC jumps to 0.9913, month T+1 lift falls to 1.65x, under the 2.0 gate, and the model's 35% share is held instead (share 65%, net -USD 260,400 to USD 277,200). Do both and nothing is live: share 0%, net -USD 307,200 to -USD 159,600. Session 3 takes the leak apart.

Real world

The data under the price has its own terms. Serai's orders are derived from the Olist public dataset, released under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, a non-commercial share-alike licence. That is fine for learning and for a bench; it is not a basis for a product sold to sellers. A real company pricing this pack would need its own order data and its own rights to it, which is one more reason the bottom layer belongs at the start of the plan: the question "whose rows, under what terms" is a data engineering question before it is a legal one.

★ Serai's gates (constructed policy)1 DE 0 blocking failures after repair 2 analytics registry agreement >= 99.5% with a control total 3 ML month T+1 lift >= 2.0x the base rate 4 gen AI rubric pass >= 70% AND golden agreement >= 85% 5 product price counts only the layers whose gate passed

Set the earliest quarter, bottom up. A row cannot start its live date before the row it consumes has passed. Where a row depends on a row two below it (gen AI on the registry), say so.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 2 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · With data engineering skipped, Serai's registry agreement on repeat rate falls to 65.14% while the model's lift only moves from 3.71x to 3.30x. Why did the registry break before the model?

Both layers read the same raw rows. A re-sent order puts a buyer straight into the repeat rate's numerator; in the model it adds a few false positives that blur the pattern without reversing it, costing one hit of 24.

2 · A sponsor wants the review-summary assistant live this quarter and the data engineering work "next year". On the dependency table, which layer do you fund first, and why?

C is half right: the summaries do quote registry numbers, but the registry is only right on rows that have passed the DE checks. Every branch of the decision tree passes through the first gate.

3 · A scorecard reads: 333 blocking failures, registry agreement 65.14%, lift 3.30x, gen AI pass 35% with 50% agreement. What is live, and what share of the price can Serai charge?

Each share is judged by its own gate: 3.30x clears the 2.0x lift gate, while 65.14% misses 99.5% and 35% misses 70%. That leaves the model's 35% live, and a net range of -USD 282,000 to USD 75,600.
