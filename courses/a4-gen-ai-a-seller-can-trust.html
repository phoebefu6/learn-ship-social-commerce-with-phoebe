Gen AI a seller can trust

A draft listing that reads well and a draft listing that is true are two different products, and only one of them can go in front of a buyer. Serai Commerce, the constructed enabler from session 1, wants three gen AI jobs: listing copy, review summaries per store, and later a seller-support agent. Tonight is how a leader knows any of them is safe to ship: a rubric that checks every draft against facts read from the rows, a golden set that checks the rubric, and an honest answer to the question every pitch deck skips, "will it sell more?"

Part 1 · covers the rubric and the golden set

A rubric and a golden set

7 min live

One check is not enough, because the check itself can be wrong. The working pattern has two layers: an automatic rubric reads every draft, and a small set of human verdicts reads the rubric. You need both numbers before you can say a gen AI layer is live.

LiveThree checks, all automatic3 min▶
CheckWhat it readsWhat it catches on Serai's drafts
Required factsThe listing's category and weight; the store's review count, mean score and share of one- or two-star scores, all recomputed from the rows at check timeA summary whose numbers do not match the store (S5)
Banned claimsTen patterns: "best", "guaranteed", "number one", "100 percent", "lowest price", "free shipping", authenticity, certification, feelings a score cannot show ("love", "rave"), absolutes ("always", "never")L2 ("best", "free shipping"), L6 ("authentic", "guaranteed"), S3 ("love")
LengthWords: 25 to 70 for a listing, 20 to 60 for a summaryNothing tonight; every draft is in range

The rubric passes 15 of 20, 75%, over Serai's constructed gate of 70%. The important word in the first row is recomputed. The rubric does not hold a copy of the facts; it reads them from the same registry the analytics layer serves. That is what makes gen AI depend on the layers below it, and Part 2 shows what happens when those rows are dirty.

LiveWho checks the checker: the golden set3 min▶

A golden set is a small batch of drafts a careful person has labelled by hand: fit to publish, or not. You run the rubric over the same drafts and count how often it agrees. On Serai's twenty, the golden verdict marks 15 fit and 5 unfit, and the rubric agrees on 18 of 20, 90%, over the 85% gate.

Two rules keep a golden set honest. Label it before you see the rubric's verdicts, or you will grade the rubric by itself. And keep it small enough that someone actually reads it: twenty is plenty to find the rubric's blind spots, far too few to quote agreement to a decimal place. One disagreement moves the agreement rate by five points.

Eval method in depth, from sampling a golden set to judging with a second model, belongs to Learn AI Evals with Phoebe. Tonight takes the leader's slice: the two numbers and what they let you sign.

Self-studyThree gen AI jobs, three different risks3 min▶

Listing copy speaks to buyers, so its risk is a false claim in public. Review summaries speak to sellers and brand teams, so their risk is a wrong number that steers a decision. A seller-support agent is a different class: it does not just write, it looks things up and hands off, and the risk moves from what it says to what it is allowed to do. OWASP's Top 10 for LLM applications lists that as LLM06, excessive agency, with three root causes: excessive functionality, excessive permissions, excessive autonomy. Its prevention list starts with minimising what the agent can reach and requiring user approval for high-impact actions.

Serai's agent gets three narrow tools (order status, refund policy, hand-off to a person) and nothing that writes. Builder session 8 builds it with golden questions; the assistant pattern itself belongs to Learn Ship Internal AI Assistant with Phoebe. For the leader the order matters: ship the two writing jobs with a rubric first, and only then let anything act.

Real world

"153 reviews" is a counting rule, not a fact. In the Olist data the satisfaction survey goes out once the customer receives the product or the estimated delivery date is due, and some orders carry more than one review: in Serai's sample, 39 orders have more than one and 68 have none. So a summary's review count depends on whether you count review rows or reviewed orders. The rubric settles it once, in the registry, and every summary is checked against that one number. Serai's summaries work from review scores only; no review text is used.

Part 2 · covers false passes, false fails and dirty facts

False pass, false fail

7 min live

The two drafts where the rubric and the human disagree are the cheapest lesson on the page. One is a draft that should never ship and passes anyway; the other is a correct draft the rubric rejects. They cost very different things.

LiveThe false pass: fluent, plausible, wrong3 min▶

L8 is a gift-box watch listing. It has the right category, the right weight, no banned word and a sensible length, so all three checks pass. It also says the watch ships from Sao Paulo, and the seller row says Rio de Janeiro state. Nobody wrote a check for origin, so the rubric cannot see it. A buyer who orders expecting a short delivery can.

NIST's Generative AI Profile has a name for the underlying behaviour, confabulation: "the production of confidently stated but erroneous or false content". OWASP's Top 10 for LLM applications files the consequence under LLM09, misinformation, which "occurs when LLMs produce false or misleading information that appears credible", and names the human half of the failure: overreliance, when users "place excessive trust in LLM-generated content, failing to verify its accuracy". Its prevention list includes automatic validation of key outputs, which is what a rubric is. The lesson of L8 is that validation only covers the facts someone listed.

LiveThe false fail: right, in the wrong format2 min▶

L4 says the tool comes in at 1.75 kg. The catalogue says 1,750 g. The draft is correct; the rubric looks for grams and fails it. A false fail is annoying rather than dangerous: an editor reads it, shrugs, and approves it. But a rubric with many false fails trains editors to approve everything it flags, and then the true fails go through with them. Count false fails as a cost, just a smaller one.

The right response to both corners is the same: change the rubric (add an origin check, read kilograms), not the gate. Lowering the 85% agreement gate to make a number look better throws away the one signal that told you where the rubric is blind.

Self-studyDirty rows break good copy: 15 of 20 becomes 73 min▶

Run the same twenty drafts with the data engineering layer skipped, so the facts are recomputed from the raw exports that still hold the constructed re-sent orders. Store 2 now has 155 reviewed orders instead of 153. A correct summary that says 153 fails its required-fact check. Across the twenty, the pass rate falls to 7 of 20 (35%) and agreement with the golden verdict falls to 50%: nine good summaries fail at once. Both gen AI gates are missed and the layer is held, which is the single biggest reason session 2's skipped-layer roadmap keeps only 35% of the product's price.

In production the failure tends to run the other way, and it is worse. The language model writes from the dirty facts, the rubric checks against the same dirty facts, and everything passes. A rubric is only as true as the rows it reads. That is why gen AI sits on layer 4 and not layer 1.

Real world

Multi-seller orders make "ships from" a real trap. In the Olist data one order can hold several items, each fulfilled by a different seller, so the seller's state belongs to the item, not to the order or the store. A draft that takes "ships from" from the wrong level reads perfectly and is wrong for some buyers. L8's mistake is constructed; the reason it is easy to make is in the real rows.

Part 3 · covers the contested evidence on AI copy and sales

Does AI-written copy sell more?

6 min live

Every gen AI pitch for sellers ends on an uplift. The honest answer is that two lines of research point in different directions because they ask different questions, and neither one tells you what Serai's sellers would see. A leader's job here is to hold the disagreement open, not to pick the number she likes.

LiveWhat the field experiments found2 min▶

Fang, Yuan, Zhang, Donati and Sarvary ran randomized field experiments on a cross-border e-commerce platform. Their abstract (version 6, read at source) reports effects "ranging from no detectable impact to 16.3%", depending on how much gen AI actually added to each workflow, with gains coming primarily through higher conversion rather than larger carts.

Two things a pitch deck will leave out. First, the top of that range belongs to a pre-sale chatbot, not to listing copy; for product descriptions an earlier version reported an effect of about 2%. That figure is reported, and the versions of the paper differ, so it is not a number to plan with. Second, it is one platform, in one setting, with that platform's buyers. It is evidence that the effect is real somewhere and varies a lot, not a forecast for Serai.

LiveWhat the survey experiments found2 min▶

Cicek, Gursoy and Lu (2024) ran experimental surveys with more than 1,000 US adults. As reported in a Washington State University press release (the paper itself was not read for these pages), products described as using artificial intelligence were less popular, and more so for high-risk products.

Read the question carefully. This is about mentioning AI in product copy, telling the buyer the product uses AI. It says nothing directly about whether copy a model drafted sells better or worse than copy a person wrote. It does carry a quieter lesson for Serai: if a listing ever advertises "AI-powered" as a feature, test that line on its own before anyone assumes it helps.

Self-studyHow to say it in a steering meeting2 min▶

The professional norm is simple: never quote a published uplift as a forecast for your own catalogue. The field experiments measured one platform's buyers; the surveys measured stated intent, not purchases. Different settings, questions and outcomes. Neither settles whether Serai's sellers would sell more with AI-written copy, and nothing on these pages resolves it.

What a leader can promise is a trust case, not a sales case: drafts that pass a rubric, a rubric that agrees with a human, and a held-out test on your own listings if anyone wants a sales number. The trust case is why the gen AI layer earns 25% of the data product's price in session 5's constructed assumptions; the sales case is something you measure after launch, not before.

Real world

The same word, two experiments. "AI listings" in the field experiments means a model helped write or answer; "AI" in the surveys means the product was described as using AI. A headline that merges them ("AI copy lowers sales" or "AI copy lifts sales 16%") is wrong both ways. When someone forwards you one of those headlines, ask which question was asked and who the buyers were.

Build-along

Your rubric and five golden verdicts

★ 20 min · everyone builds

Pick one listing type from your own catalogue: a category you sell a lot of, or one where a wrong claim would hurt. No catalogue to hand? Use Serai's garden tools listings with the facts on this page. You will write the rubric, then label five drafts by hand before running it in your head. Your rubric will differ from Serai's, which is the point.

Name the listing type and its required facts. Three to five facts a buyer relies on and that live in a row somewhere: category, weight, size, ships-from, material. For each, write which table holds it. A fact with no row is a fact the rubric cannot check, so it does not go in the copy.

★ Serai's garden tools listing, required factsListing type: garden tools Required facts (and where they live): category product catalogue, category name weight product catalogue, grams (accept kg too: see L4) ships-from seller row, state (missing from Serai's rubric: see L8) Each fact is recomputed from the rows at check time, never pasted into the rubric.

Write the banned claims. The things your legal, brand or marketplace rules already forbid, plus anything your data cannot prove. If you summarise review scores, a score cannot tell you that customers "love" anything.

★ Banned claims, starter listSuperlatives: best, number one, #1 Promises: guaranteed, 100 percent, lowest price, free shipping Unprovable: original, authentic, genuine, certified Feelings: love, loves, rave (scores say how buyers rated, not why) Absolutes: always, never, everyone

Set the length. A minimum stops a draft that says nothing; a maximum stops a draft that buries the facts. Serai uses 25 to 70 words for a listing and 20 to 60 for a summary; set your own from the listings that already sell.

Write or pick five drafts and label them by hand, now. Fit to publish or not, with one line of reason each. Make at least one of them hard: correct but oddly formatted, or fluent with one fact you cannot check. Label all five before you apply the rubric.

★ Golden verdicts, constructed exampleDraft 1 fit facts match, plain claims, 41 words Draft 2 not fit "free shipping" (banned), and we do not offer it Draft 3 fit weight given as 1.75 kg: correct, just not in grams Draft 4 not fit ships-from state does not match the seller row Draft 5 fit? hard one: says "sturdy"; is that a claim we can prove? (constructed for the exercise; your drafts and verdicts will differ)

Run the rubric in your head and fill the gen AI row. Count passes and count agreements with your five verdicts. Every disagreement is a line you add to the rubric, not a reason to move the gate. Then write the row for your roadmap.

★ The gen AI row of the roadmapLayer 4, gen AI: listing copy and review summaries for sellers. Consumes: facts recomputed from the registry (layer 2), never pasted in. Test: rubric pass rate, and rubric agreement with a hand-labelled golden set. Gate: pass >= 70% AND agreement >= 85% (Serai's constructed policy; set your own). Owner: the content lead labels the golden set; the data lead owns the facts. Promise: a trust case, not an uplift. Sales effect measured on our own listings.
✗ The gen AI row that sounds finished and checks nothing Layer 4: deploy AI copywriter across all categories. Expected +16% conversion (an industry figure). Human review where needed.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 4 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Serai's rubric passes 15 of 20 drafts and agrees with the golden verdict on 18 of 20. Which of the two disagreements matters more before launch?

A false pass reaches a buyer; a false fail reaches an editor. Both gates pass, but L8 shows a fact the rubric never checks. Fix the rubric with an origin check; never move the gate to hide the gap.

2 · A vendor cites a 16.3% sales effect for gen AI, and a colleague cites a study where AI products were less popular. What is the honest reading?

The 16.3% is the top of a range that starts at no detectable impact and belongs to a chatbot workflow (reported); the survey result, also reported, is about mentioning AI. Neither is a forecast for your catalogue.

3 · With the data engineering layer skipped, the same drafts pass 7 of 20 and agree with the golden verdict only 50% of the time. Why?

The drafts and verdicts did not change; the facts under them did. Nine good summaries fail at once. In production it usually runs the other way: drafts and rubric both read dirty facts and everything passes.
