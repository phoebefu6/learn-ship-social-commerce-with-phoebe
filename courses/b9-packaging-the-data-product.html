Part 1 · covers the pack, its gates and who may see which seller's data

What ships, and what each part waits on

6 min live

The pack has three parts, and each one is built on a different layer. Each part ships only while its layer passes its gate, and all three stand on the data engineering floor: zero blocking failures after repair. Write that dependency into the spec before writing a line of the product, because it is the thing a seller will ask about the day a number moves.

Every gate, every share and every price figure on this page is constructed for Serai; the measured values beside the gates are the ones session 10 computes on Serai's 8,614 orders. The seller-support agent from session 8 is not in the pack. It answers questions about orders and policy; it was never measured in session 10, so nothing in the price depends on it. If a part cannot name the gate it waits on, it is a feature, not a part of the product, and it carries no share.

LiveWho may see which seller's data3 min▶

A benchmark is only useful if a store can compare itself with others, and only trustworthy if no store can read another's numbers. Three rules make both true, and all three belong in the spec, not in a reviewer's memory:

  • Own rows only. A store sees its own orders, GMV and repeat rate, its own repeat list and its own copy drafts. The repeat list never carries another store's customers, even when one person buys from both.
  • Peers pooled, never named. The comparison is an aggregate over the store's segment, such as a median, with the store itself taken out of the pool.
  • A minimum pool size. When a segment holds too few other stores, a pooled number can be read back to a single competitor, so the benchmark is withheld and the page says why. The minimum is a choice you write down and defend; there is no universal number to copy.

The customer keys are already masked by the schema from session 1 and resolved by the identity gate the Enablers course builds (Analyst session 3 there). Masking keys is not the same as an access rule: a masked repeat list handed to the wrong store is still a leak of that store's customers.

Self-studyDecide what a seller is before you count them2 min▶

"Paying sellers" is the input that decides the sign of the price range in Part 3, so its unit matters. A seller could be a brand, a storefront on one marketplace, or a legal entity that runs several storefronts. Serai manages 48 brands across three marketplaces. Each definition gives a different count, a different peer pool for the benchmark and a different access boundary. Pick one, write it at the top of the spec beside the grain sentences from session 1, and use it in all three places.

Real world

One order, several sellers. The Olist dataset notes say an order may hold several items, each fulfilled by a distinct seller. A per-store benchmark built on the orders table would hand one store's items to another. That is why store lives on lines in Serai's schema, and why every per-store number in the pack is rolled up from lines: the access rule is only as good as the grain it is computed on.

Part 2 · covers the session 2 checks and registry agreement as service levels

Tests become service levels

6 min live

You already wrote the tests. A service level is a test with two things added: a threshold the buyer can read, and a consequence when it is missed. The consequence is the part teams leave out, and without it a service level is a dashboard nobody has to act on.

LiveService levels as data, beside the tests3 min▶

Keep the service levels in the same file as the spec, so a reviewer can read the promise and the test that enforces it side by side. Each entry names the test from session 2 or 3, the threshold, whether a miss blocks the load, and what the buyer sees.

★ Service levels, one entry per testSERVICE_LEVELS = [ # test threshold blocks load buyer sees on a miss ("keys_unique", "0 duplicate keys after repair", True, "no refresh today, reason shown"), ("customer_key_resolves", "100% after the crosswalk", True, "no refresh today, reason shown"), ("amounts_not_negative", "refunds quarantined", False, "nothing; adjustments table grows"), ("timestamps_ordered", "flagged, counted, published", False, "a count in the release notes"), ("registry_agreement", ">= 99.5% on all three", False, "registry part held, share off"), ]

Notice that the registry row does not block the load. The load finishes, the numbers are computed, and the part is held. Blocking the load would also stop the repeat list, which might be passing; holding the part removes only what failed.

LiveMeasure on a second path, then manage3 min▶

Registry agreement means something only because the reference totals come from a separate code path: the data builder computed 8,614 orders, BRL 1,197,842.24 and 250 of 8,333 customers repeating straight from the source rows, and the registry on the repaired frame matches all three at 100.00%. Run the registry on the raw exports and repeat-rate agreement falls to 65.14%, because each re-sent order turns a one-time buyer into a repeat one. A service level checked against itself always passes.

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework names four functions, "govern, map, measure, and manage". A service level is "measure" with a threshold someone owns; the consequence column is "manage". Write both, or the product has a number nobody has to act on.

Self-studyA service level measured on 24 events2 min▶

The repeat list's gate is month T+1 lift of at least 2.0x. That month holds 574 orders and 24 real repeats; the top decile of 58 orders caught 9, lift 3.71x. One hit more or less moves lift by 0.41. With the leaked field from session 6 the model caught 4, lift 1.65x, and the part was held. Before a share of the price rests on a service level, write next to it how many events it was measured on and how far one event moves it. A gate that one customer can flip deserves a re-check every month, not a quarterly glance.

Real world

The 122 orders that would block forever. In the real Olist rows, 122 of Serai's orders log a carrier pickup before payment approval, and 1 has no approval timestamp at all. They are a source quirk, not a pipeline bug, and they change no metric. A service level that blocked on them would stop every load until the source changed, which it will not. So they are flagged, counted and published, and the threshold says exactly that. A service level you cannot meet on real data is a service level that will be quietly switched off.

Part 3 · covers revenue, cost and net ranges and the live share

The price is a range, and the gates set its share

5 min live

Nobody knows yet how many sellers will pay, or how much. So the spec writes four assumptions as low and high values and lets arithmetic do the rest. Each layer's share of the price counts only while its gate passes. A failed gate does not discount the product; it switches that share off, and the cost does not move.

LiveFour assumptions, two ranges, one net2 min▶

Serai's four stated assumptions: 40 to 160 paying sellers, USD 150 to 350 a seller a month, a build of USD 150,000 to 240,000, and a run cost of USD 20 to 35 a seller a month. Revenue is sellers x price x 12 x live share; cost is build plus run cost x sellers x 12. The net is low revenue minus high cost, to high revenue minus low cost.

SettingLive shareFirst-year revenueFirst-year net

Cost is USD 159,600 to 307,200 in every row. That net is a corner bound: each end takes every input at its worst or best together, and the low end even charges the run cost of 160 sellers against the revenue of 40. It is deliberately wide. Its job is to show which input to test first, not to forecast a year.

Move each input alone from the value the low end uses to its other end, hold the rest, and watch the low end. The build-along prints this table; it is arithmetic on stated assumptions, nothing measured.

Paying sellers decides the sign: it moves the low end more than twice as far as any other input. And no single input flips the low end above zero; the best of them still leaves it at minus USD 19,200. So the first piece of evidence the product needs is not a better model or a cheaper build. It is a count of sellers who would pay, gathered with a written price before build money is released.

Self-studyWhy no conversion uplift goes into the price2 min▶

It is tempting to raise the price input by quoting a published sales uplift for AI-written copy. The evidence disagrees, so it cannot carry a price. Fang, Yuan, Zhang, Donati and Sarvary (2026) ran randomized field experiments on a cross-border e-commerce platform and report effects "ranging from no detectable impact to 16.3%", depending on the workflow; summaries of an earlier version put AI product descriptions at about 2% (reported; versions differ). Cicek, Gursoy and Lu (2024, reported via a university press release) found in survey experiments that products described as using AI were less popular, more so for high-risk products; that study is about mentioning AI in copy, not about AI-written copy. Different questions, settings and outcomes. Neither tells you whether Serai's sellers would sell more. Leave the uplift out of the price and measure it on your own catalogue.

Real world

The licence is a line in the spec. Serai's rows are derived from the Olist public dataset, published on Kaggle under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: non-commercial and share-alike. That is fine for learning and for proving the method on real orders. It also means a pack sold for money could not ship these rows. A commercial seller benchmark would need its own data, collected from its own sellers under their contracts, and the spec says so in its licence line before anyone quotes a price.

Build-along

The product spec, as code

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Plain Python and pandas, no download needed. Blocks 2 to 4 use only the gate values session 10 measures and Serai's four stated assumptions, so the printed lines below are what the script prints. Block 5 runs on six constructed stores, not Olist rows. Run each block in one file, in order.

Write the spec as data. One dictionary a reviewer can read without running anything. The values are Serai's constructed policy; replace them with your own.

★ Block 1 · the specSPEC = { "product": "Serai seller growth pack (constructed)", "seller": "one brand, across all three marketplaces", "contents": { "registry_tables": "orders, GMV, repeat rate per store, plus a pooled peer median", "repeat_list": "the store's own customers, ranked by chance of another order within 90 days", "copy_drafts": "listing copy and review summaries that passed the rubric", }, "definitions": { "orders": "distinct order keys after repair", "gmv": "sum of item price, no freight, no adjustments", "repeat_rate": "customers with 2+ orders / customers", }, "freshness": "refreshed after each daily load; a held part shows its last good date", "access": "own rows only; peers pooled and never named; small pools withheld", "licence": "derived Olist rows, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: non-commercial; a paid product needs its own data", } # prints nothing: this block is the contract the next four enforce

Turn measured gates into a live share. The measured values are the ones session 10 prints for each setting. A failed gate removes its whole share.

★ Block 2 · gates to live shareGATES = { "registry": lambda m: m["registry_agree_min"] >= 0.995, "model": lambda m: m["lift_t1"] >= 2.0, "genai": lambda m: m["genai_pass"] >= 0.70 and m["golden_agree"] >= 0.85, } SHARE = {"registry": 0.40, "model": 0.35, "genai": 0.25} def live_share(m): live = {layer: ok(m) for layer, ok in GATES.items()} return live, round(sum(SHARE[k] for k, on in live.items() if on), 2) settings = { "every layer built": dict(registry_agree_min=1.0000, lift_t1=3.71, genai_pass=15/20, golden_agree=18/20), "leaked field": dict(registry_agree_min=1.0000, lift_t1=1.65, genai_pass=15/20, golden_agree=18/20), "DE skipped": dict(registry_agree_min=0.6514, lift_t1=3.30, genai_pass=7/20, golden_agree=10/20), } for name, m in settings.items(): live, share = live_share(m) print(f"{name:18} {share:.0%} held: {[k for k, on in live.items() if not on]}") # prints: every layer built 100% held: [] # leaked field 65% held: ['model'] # DE skipped 35% held: ['registry', 'genai']

Turn four assumptions into three ranges. The net pairs low revenue with high cost, and back.

★ Block 3 · revenue, cost, netA = {"sellers": (40, 160), "price_month": (150, 350), "build": (150_000, 240_000), "run_seller_month": (20, 35)} def year_one(share, a=A): rev = (a["sellers"][0] * a["price_month"][0] * 12 * share, a["sellers"][1] * a["price_month"][1] * 12 * share) cost = (a["build"][0] + a["run_seller_month"][0] * a["sellers"][0] * 12, a["build"][1] + a["run_seller_month"][1] * a["sellers"][1] * 12) return rev, cost, (rev[0] - cost[1], rev[1] - cost[0]) for share in (1.00, 0.65, 0.35, 0.00): rev, cost, net = year_one(share) print(f"share {share:.0%} revenue {rev[0]:,.0f} to {rev[1]:,.0f} cost {cost[0]:,.0f} to {cost[1]:,.0f} net {net[0]:,.0f} to {net[1]:,.0f}") # prints: share 100% revenue 72,000 to 672,000 cost 159,600 to 307,200 net -235,200 to 512,400 # share 65% revenue 46,800 to 436,800 cost 159,600 to 307,200 net -260,400 to 277,200 # share 35% revenue 25,200 to 235,200 cost 159,600 to 307,200 net -282,000 to 75,600 # share 0% revenue 0 to 0 cost 159,600 to 307,200 net -307,200 to -159,600

Find the assumption that decides the sign. Move one input at a time from the value the low end uses to its other end.

★ Block 4 · what lifts the low enddef low_end(sellers_pay=40, price=150, build=240_000, run=35, sellers_served=160, share=1.0): return sellers_pay * price * 12 * share - (build + run * sellers_served * 12) base = low_end() moves = { "paying sellers 40 -> 160": low_end(sellers_pay=160), "price 150 -> 350": low_end(price=350), "build 240k -> 150k": low_end(build=150_000), "sellers served 160 -> 40": low_end(sellers_served=40), "run cost 35 -> 20": low_end(run=20), } print(f"low end today {base:,.0f}") for k, v in sorted(moves.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1]): print(f"{k:26} +{v - base:,.0f} low end {v:,.0f} {'above zero' if v > 0 else 'still below zero'}") # prints: low end today -235,200 # paying sellers 40 -> 160 +216,000 low end -19,200 still below zero # price 150 -> 350 +96,000 low end -139,200 still below zero # build 240k -> 150k +90,000 low end -145,200 still below zero # sellers served 160 -> 40 +50,400 low end -184,800 still below zero # run cost 35 -> 20 +28,800 low end -206,400 still below zero

Write the seller view, with the access rule inside it. Six constructed stores in two segments, so the withheld branch actually runs. The minimum pool size is a choice for your spec, not a standard.

★ Block 5 · own rows, pooled peersimport pandas as pd stores = pd.DataFrame({ # constructed rows, not Olist "store": ["S1", "S2", "S3", "S4", "S5", "S6"], "segment": ["home", "home", "home", "home", "toys", "toys"], "orders": [410, 220, 180, 95, 300, 60], "repeat_rate": [0.034, 0.029, 0.041, 0.022, 0.031, 0.018], }) MIN_PEERS = 3 # a choice you write in the spec, not a standard def seller_view(df, me, min_peers=MIN_PEERS): mine = df[df.store == me].iloc[0] peers = df[(df.segment == mine.segment) & (df.store != me)] if len(peers) < min_peers: return {"store": me, "repeat_rate": mine.repeat_rate, "peer_median": None, "note": f"only {len(peers)} peers, benchmark withheld"} return {"store": me, "repeat_rate": mine.repeat_rate, "peer_median": round(peers.repeat_rate.median(), 3), "peers": len(peers)} print(seller_view(stores, "S2")) print(seller_view(stores, "S5")) # prints: {'store': 'S2', 'repeat_rate': 0.029, 'peer_median': 0.034, 'peers': 3} # {'store': 'S5', 'repeat_rate': 0.031, 'peer_median': None, 'note': 'only 1 peers, benchmark withheld'}

Read the spec against the licence line. Before anyone quotes a price, check that every source the product ships allows selling it. For Serai's derived Olist rows the answer is no, and the spec says so in plain words.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Builder session 9 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · The DE layer is skipped. The model still passes at 3.30x lift; the registry and gen AI gates fail. What live share of the price does the spec allow?

A failed gate switches its share off; it does not discount it. Only the model passes, so only its 35% stays live, and the net falls to -USD 282,000 to USD 75,600 while the cost stays at USD 159,600 to 307,200.

2 · Moving each assumption alone, which one moves the low end of the net most, and does it lift it above zero?

Paying sellers from 40 to 160 lifts the low end from -USD 235,200 to -USD 19,200, more than twice any other input. No single input flips the sign, so the seller count is the assumption to test first, with a written price.

3 · Store S5 has one other store in its segment. What should its seller view show?

A "median" of one peer is that peer's number, readable by a competitor. The store still sees its own rows; the pooled comparison is withheld until the pool reaches the minimum the spec sets.
