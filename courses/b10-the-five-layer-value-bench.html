The five-layer value bench

Every build step of the track, on one screen and computed in your browser. Serai Commerce, the constructed enabler, has three marketplace exports holding 8,614 real Olist orders. The bench checks the exports, repairs them, computes the registry's metrics, trains a repeat-purchase model on months up to T and scores the month after, scores twenty pre-written listings and summaries against a rubric, and prices the data product from stated assumptions. Then it hands you two ways to break it: skip the bottom layer, or leak a field from the future.

Part 1 · covers what each rung of the bench computes

Six rungs, each consuming the last

5 min live

The ladder is the five layers plus the raw files they start from. Each rung keeps everything the rung below produced and adds one layer's computation. Read the headline number on each rung before you click the next.

LiveWhy a browser bench, and how it was checked2 min▶

A bench that runs in the page cannot hide a step in a notebook. The same engine file runs under node, and an independent pure-Python reference with the same definitions, the same starting weights and the same optimiser steps agrees with it on all 454 numbers of the ladder, normal, skipped, leaked and both, to a relative tolerance of one part in a billion. The comparison itself was tested: a planted 0.1% fault on one number is caught. On load, the bench also checks ten properties of its own, for example that the repaired frame reproduces the reference GMV to the cent and that the leak raises training AUC, then plants a fault (one re-send slipped past the repair) and confirms that three properties fail. A self-test that cannot fail measures nothing.

Self-studyThe model's seven features, and the one left out3 min▶

Prior orders by this customer (log), recency of the prior order, order value in BRL (log), freight as a share of the order, instalments, whether a voucher paid part of it, and items in the order. All are known at the moment of purchase. Review score is left out on purpose: the survey goes out once the customer receives the product or the estimated date is due, and 83 of the sample's 197 repeat orders within 90 days were placed on the same day as the order before them, before any review could exist. Using it would score the model on information it would not have had. Session 6 treats this as a timing leak.

Build-along · the bench

Climb the ladder, then break it twice

★ 26 min · everyone builds

Click the rungs in order and say out loud what you expect before the numbers appear. Nothing here is random: the rows, the defects, the outputs and the optimiser are fixed, so your numbers will match the ones in the steps.

Rung 0, the raw exports. 8,766 order rows for 8,614 real orders. The naive GMV sums price, freight and refund lines and agrees 85.04% with the reference item value; the naive repeat rate is 2.90% against 3.00%, because a person on two marketplaces counts twice.

Rung 1, the checks. Read the table by export. Export C, the smallest, is the dirtiest: 96.93% of its keys unique and 94.73% of its customer keys resolving. Export B alone carries refund lines, so its amounts pass at 98.16%. The repair drops 152 re-sends, resolves 113 keys and quarantines 64 lines; the timestamp flags stay, because they are real rows and move no metric.

Rung 2, the registry. 8,614 orders, BRL 1,197,842.24, 3.00% repeat, each at 100.00% agreement with the builder's reference. That agreement is a measured property: the reference was computed from the source rows on a separate code path, so a missed repair would show up here.

Rung 3, the model. Training AUC 0.6248 on 2,744 orders, month T+1 AUC 0.7894 on 574 orders with 24 real repeats, and lift 3.71x: 9 of those 24 in the top 58. One hit moves lift by 0.41, so read the third decimal as noise.

Rung 4, gen AI. 15 of 20 pass the rubric, and the rubric agrees with the golden verdict on 18. Find the false pass (L8 claims the watch ships from Sao Paulo; the seller is in Rio de Janeiro state, and the rubric does not check origin) and the false fail (L4 gives the weight as 1.75 kg, correct, in a unit the rubric cannot read).

Rung 5, then press "Skip the DE layer". The registry's repeat rate jumps to 4.61% and its agreement falls to 65.14%. Gen AI drops to 7 of 20 with 50% agreement: every good review summary now fails, because the facts are recomputed from rows that hold re-sends. The model survives at 3.30x. The live share of price falls to 35% and the net range to minus USD 282,000 to USD 75,600.

Restore the layer and press the break button. Training AUC jumps to 0.9913; month T+1 lift falls to 1.65x, under Serai's 2.0x gate, and the model row is held. Press both together and month T+1 AUC is 0.4922, worse than a coin, with nothing live.

Part 2 · covers the anti-lever and what it proves about order

Skip a layer and the price says so

5 min live

The anti-lever never improves the scorecard. That is its job: a bench must be able to print a worse number, or it measures nothing. Here it also shows where the damage lands, which is not where most people guess.

LiveWhy the model survived and the registry did not3 min▶

A re-sent order lands on the same day as its original, for the same customer, so on the registry it turns a one-time buyer into a repeat buyer: 384 repeat customers instead of 250. On the model it adds 48 positive labels that look like same-day repeats, which are real in Olist (83 of 197 in the sample), so the model is fed plausible noise rather than a systematic lie. Its month T+1 ranking loses one hit of 24. The honest summary is not "duplicates do not matter for ML". It is that a count is fragile in a way a ranking is not, and the layers that print counts to sellers fail first.

LiveWhy the gen AI layer failed hardest3 min▶

The summaries were written from the true facts. When the facts are recomputed on rows that hold re-sends, Store 2 has 155 reviewed orders instead of 153, and a correct summary now fails its required-fact check. On the bench, 9 good summaries fail at once. In production the failure runs the other way and is worse: the model writes from the dirty facts, the rubric checks against the same dirty facts, and everything passes. OWASP's Top 10 for LLM applications calls the user side of this overreliance, when users "place excessive trust in LLM-generated content, failing to verify its accuracy". A rubric is only as good as the facts it checks against.

Part 3 · covers the break button and the leak it plants

A field from the future

4 min live

The break button adds one column: whether the customer has any later order in the warehouse. A backfill job fills it with hindsight for every historical row. At scoring time the next order has not happened, so the column is 0 for everyone. Training sees the answer; scoring does not.

LiveLeakage, as the literature defines it3 min▶

Kaufman, Rosset, Perlich and Stitelman's 2012 paper in ACM Transactions on Knowledge Discovery from Data defines leakage as "the introduction of information about the data mining target that should not be legitimately available to mine from", and proposes a "learn-predict separation": build every training row only from what would have been known at the moment of prediction. The scikit-learn documentation makes the same point about splits: shuffled cross-validation on time-ordered data "would lead to training on future data and evaluating on past data". The bench's time split already obeys that rule; the break button shows that a split cannot save you from a column that was built with hindsight.

Self-studyWhy the leaked model does worse than no field at all2 min▶

With the field present, gradient descent puts most of the weight on it (1.389 on the standardised column, against at most 0.255 on any other) and lets the honest features drift, because they barely help once the answer is on the table. At scoring the field is a constant, so the ranking is left to the drifted weights: 4 of 24 real repeats in the top 58 instead of 9. With the anti-lever on as well, month T+1 AUC falls to 0.4922. A leak does not just inflate a number; it trains the model to ignore the signals that would have worked.

Real world

The backfill job nobody thinks of as a feature. Columns like "has a later order", "lifetime orders" or "churned flag" are usually built by a nightly job for reporting, then picked up by whoever builds the first model because they are right there in the warehouse. Ask of every feature: at what time was this value known, and would it have been known at the moment the model is asked? If nobody can answer, the column waits.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Builder session 10 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · With the DE layer skipped, the bench's repeat rate reads 4.61% instead of 3.00%. What did that?

A re-send carries a variant key for the same customer, so the registry sees two orders: 384 repeat customers instead of 250. Orphan keys push the other way, splitting one person into two. D describes the naive sum, which reads 2.90%.

2 · The break button lifts training AUC to 0.9913 and drops month T+1 lift to 1.65x. Which description of the leak is right?

The split is a clean time split; the problem is a column, not the rows. has_later_order is 1 in history wherever a later order came, and 0 for every row at scoring. That breaks Kaufman et al.'s learn-predict separation.

3 · With every layer built, the rubric passes 15 of 20 outputs but agrees with the golden verdict on 18. What is the right next step?

The gate passes, but the disagreements are the cheapest lesson on the board: L8 invents an origin the rubric never checks, L4 is right in kilograms. Fix the rubric, not the threshold.
