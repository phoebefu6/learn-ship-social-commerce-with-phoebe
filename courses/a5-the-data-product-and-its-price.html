Part 1 · covers who buys, what it costs to run, and ranges from stated assumptions

Ranges from stated assumptions

6 min live

A data product's price is a guess until sellers pay for it. The honest way to write a guess down is as a range, with every input named, so anyone in the room can point at the one they disagree with. Serai needs four inputs: how many sellers pay, what each pays a month, what the build costs and what each seller costs to serve.

LiveWho buys a seller growth pack, and for which decision2 min▶

A data product is bought by a person with a recurring decision, not by "the market". Serai's pack has three parts and each one maps to a buyer inside a brand:

Part of the packBuilt on layerWho reads itThe decision it serves
Registry numbers: orders, GMV and repeat rate, one definition each, so a brand can benchmark itself against the book2 · AnalyticsBrand managerWhere am I behind, and is it the channel or the product?
Repeat list: customers ranked by how likely they are to order again3 · Machine learningBrand CRM leadWho gets next month's limited contacts?
Copy drafts: listing copy and review summaries, scored against facts4 · Gen AIContent or catalogue leadWhat do we publish without a rewrite?

If you cannot name the buyer and the decision for a part, it is a feature someone wanted to build, and it should carry no share of the price.

LiveWhat it costs to run, not only to build2 min▶

The build is a one-off: Serai assumes USD 150,000 to 240,000 for the first year. The run cost grows with every seller served: USD 20 to 35 a seller a month for refreshing the registry, re-scoring the repeat list, generating and checking copy, and answering the seller who asks why a number moved. At 160 sellers and USD 35 that is USD 67,200 a year on top of the build.

Two things follow. A run cost per seller means more sellers never make the product free to operate. And the run cost does not fall when a layer's gate fails: if the registry is held, Serai still pays to compute it, test it and explain why it is not shipping.

Self-studyRead the range before you trust it3 min▶

Builder session 10 prints the net as "low revenue minus high cost, to high revenue minus low cost". That is a corner bound: each end takes every input at its own worst or best at the same time. It is deliberately wide. The low end even charges the run cost of 160 sellers against the revenue of 40, a pairing no real year produces, so treat minus USD 235,200 as a floor nobody should plan to hit, not as a scenario.

Also notice the top of the seller range. 160 paying sellers is more than three times Serai's own book of 48 brands, so the high end quietly assumes buyers Serai does not manage today. An assumption that big belongs on the sheet in plain words, not inside a number.

A range is not a forecast and its midpoint is not one either. Its job is to show which input to test first. Part 3 finds it.

Real world

The licence is a pricing input too. Serai's rows are derived from the Olist public dataset, published on Kaggle under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: non-commercial and share-alike. That is fine for learning and for proving the method. It means a product sold for money could not ship these rows; a real seller growth pack would be built on Serai's own sellers' data, under their contracts. Check the licence of every source before the price sheet goes to finance, because a source you cannot sell from has a price of zero.

Part 2 · covers each layer's share of the price, switched by its gate

Price by live layers

6 min live

The pack has three parts and each one depends on a different layer. So Serai splits the price: the registry carries 40%, the repeat list 35% and the copy drafts 25%. A part only counts toward the price while its layer passes its gate. A failed gate does not discount the product; it switches that share off.

LiveWhat skipping data engineering does to the price3 min▶

Session 2 showed the damage: with the raw exports feeding everything, the registry's repeat rate reads 4.61% and agrees with the reference only 65.14% of the time, and gen AI passes 7 of 20 outputs with 50% golden agreement. Both gates fail. The model survives at 3.30x lift, so only its 35% stays live.

SettingLive shareFirst-year revenueFirst-year net
Every layer built100%USD 72,000 to 672,000-USD 235,200 to USD 512,400
Leaked field (model held)65%USD 46,800 to 436,800-USD 260,400 to USD 277,200
DE layer skipped35%USD 25,200 to 235,200-USD 282,000 to USD 75,600
Both at once0%USD 0-USD 307,200 to -USD 159,600

The cost column never moves: USD 159,600 to 307,200 in every row. Skipping the bottom layer saves none of the product's cost, because the run cost is per seller served. It removes revenue in proportion to the share held, which is why the top of the range falls by USD 436,800 and the bottom by only USD 46,800. The upside is what a skipped layer costs most.

LiveWhy switch off, rather than discount2 min▶

A discount says "the numbers are a bit worse". A held share says "we will not sell this part until it passes". The second is the one a seller can trust: a registry that agrees with its control total 65.14% of the time is not a cheaper registry, it is a wrong one, and a review summary quoting a count the rows do not support is the confident, erroneous content NIST's Generative AI Profile calls confabulation.

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework names four functions, "govern, map, measure, and manage". The share switch is "measure" wired to "manage": a measured number, a threshold someone owns, and a commercial consequence when it is missed. That consequence is what makes the gate real to the people who would otherwise ship anyway.

Self-studyA gate measured on 24 repeat buyers2 min▶

The model's 35% rests on a month T+1 lift of at least 2.0x. That month holds 574 orders and 24 real repeats; the top decile of 58 orders caught 9 of them, lift 3.71x. With 24 positives, one hit moves lift by 0.41. With the leaked field the model caught 4, lift 1.65x, and its share was held. Before a share depends on a gate, ask how many events the gate was measured on and how far one event moves it. If the answer is "a lot", write a second month into the gate before you write the share into a contract.

Real world

Store 2 has 153 reviews, or 155. On the repaired rows, Serai's Store 2 has 153 reviews with a mean score of 3.9 and 18% at one or two stars; on the raw exports it reads 155, because re-sent orders bring their reviews with them. The review scores are real Olist scores; the summaries are pre-written and constructed. A good summary that quotes 153 now fails a check that reads 155 from dirty rows, and every summary like it fails the same way. That is how 25% of a price disappears without anyone writing a bad sentence.

Part 3 · covers which assumption moves the low end most

The assumption that decides the sign

6 min live

The range crosses zero, so somewhere in the four inputs is the one that decides whether the first year makes or loses money. Find it by moving each input, alone, from the value the low end uses to its other end, and watching how far the low end moves. This is arithmetic on Serai's stated assumptions, nothing measured.

LiveBreak-even in sellers, the number to take to the room3 min▶

Turn the winning input into a threshold. Hold one seller count on both sides of the sum, so the sellers who pay are the sellers you serve, and ask how many it takes to cover the build. Each seller adds price minus run cost, times 12, a year.

SettingEach seller adds a yearBuild to coverBreak-even sellers
All live, worst inputs (USD 150, run 35)(150 - 35) x 12 = USD 1,380USD 240,000174
All live, best inputs (USD 350, run 20)(350 - 20) x 12 = USD 3,960USD 150,00038
DE skipped, worst inputs (35% of USD 150)(52.50 - 35) x 12 = USD 210USD 240,0001,143
DE skipped, best inputs (35% of USD 350)(122.50 - 20) x 12 = USD 1,230USD 150,000122

Arithmetic on the stated assumptions, rounded up; your own will differ. With every layer live, Serai breaks even somewhere between 38 and 174 paying sellers, and the top of that sits above the 160 the range allows. Skip data engineering and the band moves to 122 to 1,143 sellers, against a book of 48 brands. That is the skip-DE anti-lever in one sentence a finance partner understands.

LiveTest the seller count before anything else2 min▶

Price, build and run cost are worth negotiating, but none of them moves the low end as far as the number of sellers who pay. So the first test is a buyer test, not a build: put the pack in front of a handful of Serai's brands with a written price inside the USD 150 to 350 band and count who signs a letter of intent. Each signature is evidence on the input that decides the sign. A faster model or a cheaper build is evidence on inputs that do not.

Self-studyWhy no conversion uplift goes into the price2 min▶

It is tempting to raise the price input by quoting a published sales uplift for AI-written copy. Session 4 showed why not: the evidence disagrees. Fang et al. (2026) ran randomized field experiments on a cross-border e-commerce platform and report effects "ranging from no detectable impact to 16.3%" depending on the workflow; Cicek et al. (2024, reported) found in survey experiments that mentioning AI in product copy lowered purchase intent. Different questions, settings and outcomes. Neither tells you what Serai's sellers will pay, so the price input stays an assumption you test with buyers.

Real world

One order, several sellers. In the Olist data an order may hold several items, each fulfilled by a different seller. Before "paying sellers" goes on a price sheet, decide what a seller is: a brand, a storefront on one marketplace, or a legal entity that may run several. The answer can move the count more than any pricing debate, and the count is the input that decides the sign.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Skipping data engineering moves Serai's first-year net from -USD 235,200 to USD 512,400 down to -USD 282,000 to USD 75,600. Why does the top end fall so much further than the bottom?

With only the model's 35% live, revenue falls by 65% at both ends: USD 436,800 off the top, USD 46,800 off the bottom. Cost is unchanged in every setting. The model is the one layer that stays live, and no seller count changes in the arithmetic.

2 · Moved alone from the value the low end uses to its other end, which input lifts the low end of Serai's net the most?

Paying sellers adds USD 216,000 and brings the low end to -USD 19,200. Price adds USD 96,000, build USD 90,000, run cost USD 28,800. None flips the sign alone, which is why the first test is a buyer test.

3 · With the backfilled field in the model, training AUC is 0.9913 but month T+1 lift is 1.65x, under the 2.0 gate. What happens to the price?

A failed gate switches its share off, it never discounts it, and training AUC is not the gate: the month the model never saw is. The registry and copy drafts keep their 40% and 25%.
